### Terms this paper uses (one place, so they need not be reconstructed)

| term | what it means here |
|---|---|
| **arm** | one member of a paired comparison. The **baseline** arm follows the published convention; the **strategy** arm is the intervention under test (e.g. a learning-rate change). "Baseline" does **not** mean unmodified software — both arms run the same code |
| **cell** | one (corpus pair × arm) unit of the design. A cell name such as `shwd2sf` reads **source→target** (SHWD → SFCHD); the corpora are `dota15`, `aitod`, `visdrone`, `smoke`, `shwd`, `sfchd`, `mende`, `mask` |
| **`best` / `last`** | the two checkpoints compared throughout: `best.pt`, written by the training script by **fitness** on the monitored split, and the final epoch |
| **selection premium** | `best − last` **within a run**: what selecting the best checkpoint adds, measured on the split the numbers are reported from |
| **Δgap** | the paired **difference of the premium between the two arms**, per corpus. Positive means `val` **understates** the strategy arm's effect (§5.1, Tier 2) |
| **realization rate** | `prem_test / prem_val`, aggregated over cells (§8.6); the share of the premium selected on `val` that survives on a disjoint `test` |
| **κ** | through-origin slope of `ΔT` on `ΔV` over a run's epochs (§8.7): how much of a run's movement on `val` appears on `test` |
| **same directory / alias** | a dataset config whose `val:` and `test:` point at the same path, so the checkpoint is selected on the split it is scored on |
| **`shapeiou`** | the shape-aware IoU variant used in the registered replication's strategy arm (§9) |
| **`sio_b_results.csv`** | the per-run results table of the clean-protocol batch; its unwritten endpoint convention is disclosed as a defect in §10 |
| **FROZEN-HASH** | the registration's self-referential stamp: the md5 of the registration text above the stamp line (§9, verifiable by re-hashing that text) |

## S11 · Formalisation: propositions and proofs (companion to §13 of the article)

Notation as in §13 of the article: V(e) = M_{S_val}(W(e)), T(e) = M_{S_test}(W(e)), ê = argmax_e V(e),
e_f = the final checkpoint, premium_val = V(ê) − V(e_f), premium_test = T(ê) − T(e_f).

### Proposition 1 (non-identifiability of the raw gap)

**Statement.** V(ê) − T(ê) = [V(e_f) − T(e_f)] + [(V(ê) − V(e_f)) − (T(ê) − T(e_f))]; hence for any
candidate difficulty term δ′ the residual o′ = G − δ′ reproduces the observation exactly, and no
estimator using only the endpoint pair (ê, e_f) identifies δ or o separately.

**Proof.** Substitute V(ê) = V(e_f) + (V(ê) − V(e_f)) and T(ê) = T(e_f) + (T(ê) − T(e_f)); the two
expressions are equal identically. For the second claim, fix any δ′ ∈ ℝ and set o′ = G − δ′; then
δ′ + o′ = G, which is the only equation the data impose. ∎

**Remark (which assumption buys identifiability).** Differencing two arms with matched seeds gives
Δgap = E[G_base − G_strat] = E[(δ_base − δ_strat) + (o_base − o_strat)]. The pairing assumption is
E[δ_base − δ_strat] = 0, which holds when the two arms are trained and evaluated on one corpus and one
split pair. Under an aliasing configuration both arms' δ are maxima of the *same* split's noise, so
the assumption fails by construction: this is the formal content of §9.4.

### Proposition 2 (exact three-component identity)

**Statement.** Δgap = Δδ_f + Δprem_val − Δprem_test, where Δδ_f = (V(e_f) − T(e_f))_base −
(V(e_f) − T(e_f))_strat, Δprem_val = (premium_val)_base − (premium_val)_strat, and Δprem_test is the
same for the realized premiums.

**Proof.** Apply Proposition 1 to each arm and subtract; the split-difficulty terms do not cancel but
combine into Δδ_f, and the bracket terms are Δprem_val − Δprem_test. ∎

**Verification.** The identity holds to the printed digit on all 13 scanned cells (Table S10) and on
both escalated cells at n = 10: −1.226 = (−1.583) + (+0.090) − (+0.267) and
−3.315 = (−7.072) + (−0.213) − (+3.971) (`work/x1_aux_20260918.txt`).

### Proposition 3 (the sign of Δgap does not identify a mechanism)

**Statement.** If the three components of Proposition 2 are of the same order, then for every target
sign s ∈ {+1, −1} there exist admissible component values whose sum has sign s; hence the observed
sign does not determine which component dominates.

**Proof.** Let the components be a, b, c with |a|, |b|, |c| ≤ K. Fixing b and c, choose a = +2K to
obtain a positive sum and a = −2K for a negative one; both lie in the allowed range. ∎

**Empirical support.** Measured medians of the three components are **1.07 / 0.54 / 0.93 pp** (same
order), the 13-cell scan contains **both signs** (ten positive, three negative; −2.46 to +13.66 pp),
and candidate mechanisms M1/M2/M4/M5 are each refuted by sign mismatches (Table S9). **Two
corrections of record.** ① An earlier version of the companion script tried to demonstrate
reachability by resampling *cells*; that inherits the cells' own sign split (98.4 % negative on the
four cells used) and tests nothing. ② A stronger claim — that the sign is *fragile* — is **not
supported**: perturbing one arm-difference term by ±10 % flips the sign in **0 %** of cells
(±50 %: 23 %), with a median cancellation ratio of **0.485**. The proposition is therefore stated as
"the dominant term varies by corpus and the sign is a sum of same-order terms", which is what the
data support (`work/theory_p3a_correct_20260919.txt`).

### Proposition 4 (realization rate = 1 − winner's-curse share)

**Statement.** Assume V(e) = v(e) + ε_e and T(e) = v(e) + τ + η_e with E[ε] = E[η] = 0, ε, η
independent of v, and η independent of the selection event {ê = e}. Then
E[premium_test] = E[premium_val] − WC with WC := E[ε_ê − ε_{e_f}] ≥ 0, so
realization rate := E[premium_test] / E[premium_val] = 1 − WC / E[premium_val] ≤ 1.

**Proof.** (i) E[premium_test] = E[T(ê) − T(e_f)] = E[v(ê) − v(e_f)] + E[η_ê − η_{e_f}]; the second
term is 0 by the stated independence and mean-zero assumption. (ii) E[premium_val] = E[V(ê) − V(e_f)]
= E[v(ê) − v(e_f)] + E[ε_ê − ε_{e_f}]. (iii) Subtracting (i) from (ii) yields
E[premium_test] = E[premium_val] − E[ε_ê − ε_{e_f}]. (iv) Since ê maximises V = v + ε, the selected
checkpoint carries positive noise in expectation, so E[ε_ê − ε_{e_f}] ≥ 0; the equality case is
selection on the signal alone. ∎

**Which assumption carries which half.** The inequality (≤) needs only "ê is chosen using V" — that
is exactly the convention under audit, so the *direction* is robust. The closed form additionally
needs η to stay out of the selection; under aliasing (`val` and `test` the same split) the two noises
coincide and the closed form does not apply — which is why §9.4 treats the aliased cells' realization
figure as a magnitude statement about bias, not as mechanism evidence.

**Tests on the archive** (`work/theory_p4_test_20260919.py`, `work/theory_meta_and_tests_20260919.py`):

| test | result |
|---|---|
| inequality per run (WC > 0) | **+0.463 pp**, t = 8.34, **p = 1.8×10⁻¹²**; realized premium smaller in **68 / 81** runs (sign test p = 3.8×10⁻¹⁰) |
| rate vs `val` noise (terciles) | **65.6 % → 49.8 % → 13.8 %**; Spearman **ρ = −0.466, p = 1.3×10⁻⁵, n = 80** |
| premium level vs noise (σ√(2 ln E)) | **withdrawn**: slope 0.026 ± 0.222, p = 0.82, n = 101 — the premium's level is set by the curve's shape, not its jitter |

### Proposition 5 (family dependence of Benjamini–Hochberg)

**Statement.** For a fixed multiset of p-values and level q, BH's rejection set is determined by the
step-up condition on i/m·q; consequently the rejection count is not monotone under adding hypotheses:
adding sufficiently small p-values raises the thresholds behind them and can turn a previously
non-rejected hypothesis into a rejected one.

**Proof.** BH rejects H_(1..k) for the largest k with p_(k) ≤ (k/m)q. Since (k/m)q is increasing in k,
inserting new small p-values shifts existing ones to higher ranks k, which relaxes their thresholds;
a hypothesis that failed at rank j can therefore pass at rank j + t. ∎

**Instance in this paper.** Adding the two escalated cells' n = 10 p-values (6.5×10⁻⁹, 1.3×10⁻⁸) moved
`shwd2sf` from "does not survive" to "survives" (0.0053 against a line of 0.01154), and the 13-cell
count went from **0 to 3** (`work/recompute_bh_20260917.py`). Declaring the family is therefore a
precondition for the count to be meaningful, not a presentational choice.

### Corollary to Proposition 4 (shape v. noise) — derivation and measurements

**Derivation (exact).** For one arm and one budget E let `V_r(e)` be run r's `val` metric at epoch e,
`premium_r = max_e V_r(e) − V_r(E)`, and `m(e) = mean_r V_r(e)`. Put
`shape(E) = max_e m(e) − m(E)` and `ν_r = premium_r − shape(E)`. Then
`premium_r = shape(E) + ν_r` **by construction**: no assumption about the noise distribution is used.
Two immediate consequences: `shape` is a functional of the mean curve alone and therefore **independent
of σ**; and `E[ν_r] ≥ 0`, because replacing each run's curve by the mean curve can only lower the
maximum — this is the `val`-side member of the winner's-curse family of Proposition 4.

**Measurement 1 — the premium's level is shape, and the noise term is flat in E** (same corpus,
pretraining, two arms, five seeds; only the epoch budget changes; `work/prop6_shape_noise_20260920.py`):

| E | premium (mean of arms) | `shape` | `shape`/premium |  |  |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | +0.444 | — | 71.2 % |  |  |
| 100 | +0.682 | — | 90.5 % |  |  |
| 200 | +0.971 | — | 87.7 % |  |  |
| 400 | +0.907 | — | 9.2 % |  |  |

**Four-budget outcome (pre-registered criteria C1–C3).** C1 — monotone rise of the premium with E — is **not met**: the series peaks at E = 200 and falls at E = 400 (four of the ten E = 400 runs stopped early under the protocol's own `patience`, at effective budgets 388/318/362/375; on the six full runs the E = 400 mean is +1.278 pp). C2 — the σ√(2 ln E) form — is **not rejected**: premium(400)/premium(100) = 1.329 against the predicted 1.141, inside the pre-declared band [0.86, 1.43]. C3 — the shape control — **passes** (ê/E 0.833 → 0.815).

E = 50 → 200: `shape` ×2.70, premium ×2.19, `sd(ν)` ×0.92; the σ√(2 ln E) form would predict ×1.24.
The pre-registered decisive test of that form is criterion C2 (band [0.86, 1.43] on
premium(400)/premium(100)); its verdict is reported in §S31–S33 and in §13.1.

**Measurement 2 — where the realized gain goes** (40 runs with per-epoch `test` readouts on a 5-epoch
grid, `val` from each run's own `results.csv`; selection on `val` only, readout on `test` only):

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| premium(`val`) | **+0.981 pp** |
| premium(`test`), oracle peak on `test` | +0.704 pp (**71.7 %** of the `val` premium) |
| premium(`test`), using the `val` argmax | **+0.211 pp (21.5 %)** |
| runs whose `val` and `test` peaks agree | **17.5 %** |
| median \|ê_val − ê_test\| | 10 epochs (grid step 5) |

So the low realization rate is a **peak-alignment** phenomenon, not an absence of gain: an oracle that
could pick the `test` peak would realize 71.7 % of the `val` premium, and `val`-selection retains 30 %
of that.

**Measurement 3 — selection rules** (same 40 runs; every rule chooses on `val`, readout on `test`):

| rule | realized `test` premium | captured share | cost to the reported number | within one step of the `test` peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| argmax (current convention) | +0.211 | **30.0 %** | 0.000 | 45.0 % |
| mean of top-2 `val` epochs | +0.406 | 57.7 % | 0.690 | 55.0 % |
| mean of top-3 `val` epochs | +0.380 | 54.0 % | 0.596 | 72.5 % |
| mean of top-5 `val` epochs | +0.490 | **69.6 %** | 0.727 | 62.5 % |
| argmax of a 5-epoch moving average | +0.459 | 65.2 % | 0.526 | 70.0 % |
| final epoch (no selection) | +0.000 | 0.0 % | 0.981 | 2.5 % |

**What these measurements are not.** They are a **post-hoc comparison on the same runs**, not a
pre-registered experiment: the rules were evaluated after the runs existed. The grid is 5 epochs wide,
so "within one step" means ±5 epochs; all choices are made on `val` and every readout is on the other
split; and the oracle row is an upper bound, not an attainable protocol.

**A candidate mechanism for the plateau rule — stated as a candidate, not as a result.** The natural
explanation of Measurement 3 is that the argmax of a noisy curve is pulled towards epochs whose noise
happens to be high, so its underlying value sits below the true peak (the classical peak-shift penalty,
of order σ²/|v″|), while a top-k average estimates the peak *region* with lower variance. That story
makes a sharp prediction: the plateau advantage should **grow with the noise of the `val` curve**. We
tested it, and it is **not established**:

* noise estimator: `σ̂` = mean over epochs of the cross-seed standard deviation at that epoch
  (`V_r(e) − mean_r V_r(e)`), i.e. the run-to-run dispersion the ten-seed design is about — not the
  residual around a smoothing of one run's own curve (which mixes curvature into "noise" and, with that
  estimator, gives ρ = +0.04, p = 0.79);
* per run (n = 40): ρ(`σ̂`, plateau − argmax) = **+0.309, p = 0.052**;
* per cell (n = **4** cells, the honest counting unit): ρ = **+0.800**, slope **+0.406 ± 0.101** per pp of
  `σ̂`, r = **+0.943**, but **p = 0.057**;
* the four cells read: `σ̂` 0.629 → +0.194 pp, 0.639 → +0.154 pp, 1.125 → +0.318 pp, 1.201 → +0.447 pp —
  i.e. both noisy cells show about twice the advantage of both quiet cells, with no exceptions.

**What this is and is not.** The *advantage* of the plateau rule is established (Measurement 3, and the
paired test +0.278 pp, t = 3.678, p = 7.1×10⁻⁴, positive in 26 of 40 runs). The *mechanism* is
**consistent but not significant on four cells**, so we report it as a candidate and not as a result.
Settling it needs more corpus cells with per-epoch `test` readouts — the direction of the effect is
already pinned down by the four we have, but four points cannot carry a significance claim.

**same val annotation file** through a ground-truth-free `ImageFolder`. This is why the reported-layer count is what it is: where annotations are not public, the default
path is to report on whichever split can be scored locally, and that split is usually also the
selection split. **COCO sits on the boundary** — its `test-dev` is server-scored and cannot be
selected on, so we count its community-reported number as independent, while a paper's own
ablation numbers are not; §6.1 states both readings and both counts. **On benchmarks like COCO the
coincidence is not an author's oversight — annotation availability pushes everyone onto the same
split**, the default path itself.

**(C-2, narrowing) But "select the checkpoint on that split" is not the shared default — only the YOLO
lineage does it.** Ultralytics (`best.pt`) and YOLOX (`best_ap`) select by the validation metric;
Detectron2 (`EVAL_PERIOD = 0`), MMDetection (no `save_best`), DETR (no best tracking) and
PaddleDetection (none found) **report the final epoch**. §5's selection-premium claim therefore reads:
**"within the pipeline family that selects by the validation metric by default (the YOLO lineage), the
convention systematically shifts arm-to-arm comparison"** — **not** "the default of the field". The
abstract and the §2.1 table claim about YOLO distributions only, consistent with this.

**Four limitations of this section** (so it is not read as a census): ① we read **default
configurations**, which users may change; ② only **5 frameworks / 23 files** — **fixed-point sampling,
not a census**; ③ the pins are **default-branch heads** and will move, hence the SHA-256 and commit per
file; ④ as in §4 and §6, a **configuration-level audit — no dataset was downloaded**.

### 8.1 The protocol
Split the pool three ways — a training subset, a **carved validation** subset for selection, a **test**
subset for reporting; carved and test are disjoint, so the reported number is no
longer the maximum a selection rule saw. Three requirements make it trustworthy:
**carve out of the pool** (the test split was never in training), **verify all three intersections are
zero** (by filename), and **freeze the endpoint definition in writing** — which checkpoint counts as "the"
reported number, whose omission is defect 3 in §10 and the source of the conflict in §7.3.

### 5.6 Why Δgap's sign varies by corpus — measurement detail
§5.1 Tier 2 reports the **fact**; with the archived quantities
**the mechanism is not identifiable**, though three candidates are **refuted** and two unsupported.

**Data**: 13 three-way corpus cells × 2 arms (paired seeds n = 3 or 10). Script
`work/gap_mechanism_20260916.py` → `gap_mechanism_20260916.txt`; the data path matches §5.4's and
every cell's Δgap agrees **value by value** with the §5.1 Tier 2 table.

> **Tables S8–S10** → Supplementary §S8.


**First, an identity**: Δgap = (arm difference on val) − (arm difference on test), hence
**Δgap > 0 ⟺ the relative arm difference on `val` is smaller than on `test`**. It compares the two splits
only and **points to no generating cause**.


**An exact decomposition** (an identity explaining the unstable sign — **not** a mechanism).
Because `M_val(ê) = V_final + premium_val`, Δgap expands into three components, **all computable from the
archive**:

> Δgap = (arm difference at the **final** epoch on val) + (**val-side selection gain** difference) − (test-side arm difference)


This is an **identity, not a fit**: for all 13 cells the "check" column (the sum of the three components)
**equals** Δgap. The three components' median magnitudes are **1.07 / 0.54 / 0.93 pp — the same order**, so
**any one of them can flip Δgap's sign**, consistent with §5.1's instability.

**The most informative cell is `mask20`**: the baseline leads the strategy by **+15.78 pp** at the **final
epoch on val**, but the strategy's **val-side selection gain** is **13.10 pp** larger; the two nearly
cancel, leaving Δgap **+1.75** (p = 0.66, not significant). **This cell shows that selection alone
can absorb an arm difference of order 13 pp** — it supports "the convention changes arm-to-arm comparison"
(§5.1 Tier 1) while showing that Δgap's sign **cannot** be used to infer any single cause.

**What this section claims.** Δgap's sign **varies by corpus** is firm (§5.1). Its generating **mechanism is
identifiable in this archive only in part**: separating "the two splits' difficulty" from "selection optimism
(one part on val, one on test)" requires **test metrics at non-selected epochs**, and the archive stores only
the test value of the **selected** checkpoint — supplying it means re-running the test evaluation (see §8.6 and §10, defect 5, for what that closed).
**What remains is bounded and stated, not left as "mechanism to be explained".**
