Object-detection papers report a number from a split they call held out. We audit that premise, with three results.

**1. The convention is broken in our own corpora.** **6 of 14** corpora we hold give the reported number no independent held-out split: the checkpoint was selected there.
Training and evaluation do not overlap: **checkpoint selection**, not contamination.

**2. The direction varies by corpus.** The premium is **positive in all four cells** (§5.1, an escalated four-cell base; **near-definitional**)
the baseline arm gaining more, the paired contrast changing sign by corpus; a declared 13-cell family leaves **3** after Benjamini–Hochberg; in 19 public benchmarks the reported number
can come from a non-independent split (§6.1 — **one rater's judgement, not a rate**), yet every unit yields a defensible count. **Changing the metric keeps the mean direction; one cell loses
significance and sign unanimity** (`smoke2sf` **+0.063 pp**, p = 0.54, **6+/5−**).

**3. Partial realization on a disjoint split.** Separating the selection set from the reported set, the
premium is realized only in part: the **claim-level interval includes zero** ([−17.3 %, 53.2 %], P = 0.118), so it is a **descriptive five-cell statement**, not a significant one; arm-cell median **17.6 %** (span **−28.5 % to +73.7 %**, **2/5 negative**),
run-weighted **23.3 %** (mAP50 **31.5 %**, **1/5** negative), run-level [8.4 %, 37.4 %] (P = 0.002), leave-one-out **9.77 %–34.16 %**.
Data-order replication lowers the mean **9 %–29 %** (§8); no power analysis was pre-registered, so the
nine `n = 3` cells stay descriptive.

```text
Dear Editor,

We submit for consideration in Pattern Recognition the manuscript
"Configuration-level split aliasing in object-detection benchmarks: how the reported
split is selected on, how far the selection transfers, and what a three-way protocol
reveals".

What the paper claims. Object-detection papers report a metric on a split they call
held out. For the YOLO-lineage pipelines we audit, a configuration that binds `val:`
and `test:` to one directory means the reported checkpoint was selected on the split
it is reported on, so the reported number is a maximum rather than a sample. We
quantify the resulting selection premium, measure how much of it survives an
independent split, and show that the bias direction is not single-valued: it varies
by corpus. We also introduce four counting units for the question "how often does this
happen?", because the same question has four all-correct answers.

Relationship to a companion manuscript. A companion manuscript by the same authors —
a budgeted fine-tuning study that this paper re-measures — uses the same experimental
archive and the same project-internal process tools. To keep the two papers
separable: this paper claims the derivation of the three-way protocol, the selection
premium and its consequences; the two "clean-protocol" readings of the SFCHD cell
(+1.434 / +0.507 pp) are first reported in the companion manuscript and are cited and
marked as such here (see §7.2). The shared tooling is disclosed and is not claimed as
a methodological contribution of either paper. Both manuscripts are under separate
consideration; neither duplicates the other's claims.

Fit to the journal. The paper is an evaluation-validity study of a pattern-recognition
task family, it reports a recomputable protocol rather than a new architecture, and
every quantitative claim carries a pointer to a local artifact (audit anchors and
checkers are listed in the Supplementary Material).

We confirm this manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and
that all authors approve submission. We declare no competing interests.

Sincerely,
[Corresponding author]
```
